Keyboard

32 keys, always. One AKM320 — spine half A and half B, 16 sensor feet each. Drop a key into a gap and whites come off the right-hand end to pay for it; everything then re-widens so the keyboard still ends exactly at the spine.

Key Type Categories — drag these

Every chip is drawn at one width; the real width comes from the keyboard when it lands. A split pair is one component — it drops both halves, and clicking either half removes both.

Key widths — drag the dividers

The bar is the four classes drawn to scale against one another. Drag a divider to trade width between two of them; the size solve then re-fits the whole keyboard, so widening one class narrows the others rather than running off the spine. An accidental stops at 0.86× a white — past that the white has no rear left to cut away for it. reset to the sheet's law

One key at a time. The bar sets the law; a single key can still break it. Hover a key's edge down in the strip and it becomes a divider you can drag, the way a window's edge is. Between two whites the drag trades width between exactly those two — one widens, its neighbour pays — so the keyboard still ends at the spine. On an accidental's own two sides it grows or shrinks about its centre, eating into the rear of the whites either side until they have no rear left to give. reset every key to its class
